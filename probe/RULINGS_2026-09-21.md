## Ruling 4: tallies are operational metadata, not probe data

The scheduled push writes day-level tallies into commit subjects
("Verdicts 2026-09-17: 3 CLEAN, 1 TRANSIENT, 1 ECHO_CHANGE"),
and those subjects are visible on origin to anyone who touches
the repository, including an operator merging an unrelated PR.
Adopted with this note, for this record going forward: such
tallies are operational metadata, not probe data in the sense of
a pin's validity condition.

What a tally supports, stated exactly rather than left for a
reviewer to find. A tally names no model and no item, and in
general it cannot determine an item's HOME or AWAY state. The
general rule has a sharp exception, and the governed item sits
inside it: against a unanimous point-mass baseline, observed TVD
is one minus the modal option's observed share, so a band at or
below 0.4 makes in-band entail a modal share of at least 0.6,
which entails unique modal match. For such items, in-band
entails HOME, and an all-CLEAN tally for a model determines
every such item of that model HOME for the day. Concretely:
with the 1 ECHO_CHANGE attributable to the halted deepseek arm
(ECHO_CHANGE_2026-09-10.md; disposition adopted 2026-09-12),
the 2026-09-15 and 09-16 subjects ("4 CLEAN, 1 ECHO_CHANGE")
determine eq_disclosure_timing HOME on both days, and the
09-17 subject bounds its away count at one, since its single
TRANSIENT may or may not be gpt-5.6-terra's.

The counter-reading is therefore recorded rather than argued
away: a reader who treats tally subjects as probe data should
grade every pin merged after its post-date tallies were pushed
as attestation-grade at best, and for the 2026-09-14 pin should
read the exposure bound in
REGISTRATION_ATTESTATION_2026-09-21.md. This ruling pins the
convention for this record; it does not claim that reader is
wrong.
