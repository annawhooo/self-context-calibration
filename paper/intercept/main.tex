\title{Judgment-Layer Drift, No Changelog:\\
56 Days Monitoring Five LLM APIs}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Blind submission}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Defenders track}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The behavior of a served LLM changes behind a stable model id, and the
change has a shape. I ran a calibrated behavioral monitor against five
production LLM APIs daily for 56 days. The monitor combines a fixed
68-item security judgment bank, $K{=}10$ samples per item against a
frozen baseline, simulation-calibrated alarm bands, and a same-day
rerun on every breach. Four findings. First, judgment behavior
oscillates rather than drifts: models revisit exact prior answer
distributions on contested items, one item returning to the identical
unanimous vector on four of its five breach days. Second, the movement
concentrates where judgment is contested. All 75 breach entries landed
on model-item slots drawn from the 23 items authored so that
practitioners split; the 45 items with a derivable riskiest option
produced no breach in 11,057 slot-days. The count is not the evidence,
since the null expects 92 breaches and the record holds 75; the
structure is. The 75 entries pile onto thirteen slots where the null
expects one at that depth, and ten of those thirteen moved toward the
same option at every breach, against 1.6 expected. Third, every
serving covariate visible from outside stayed constant while behavior
moved, except the identity string itself: one vendor aliased the
pinned id mid-study with no signal at the API surface, and only the
response-side echo (the model id each reply reports) caught it. Even
that was a detection, not an explanation. From outside the API, a
customer can detect that a served model changed, but they cannot say
why. Fourth, the movement has a per-model shape. Slots that never
breached sit within 0.011 mean total variation distance (TVD) of what
sampling arithmetic predicts on all five models, while the breached
threads run $+0.086$ to $+0.137$; ambient drift is zero at this
resolution and every detected movement belongs to a named, recurring
thread. A snapshot evaluation certifies the day it ran, not the period
it is cited for. The monitor bills about 20 USD per month. The
instrument, the record, the analysis, and every correction are
reproducible from a public artifact, apart from the row-level figures
marked in the text.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An agent pipeline is qualified against the model serving it, and
nothing announces when that stops being true. Vendors change weights,
quantization, serving configuration, and safety layers behind stable
model ids, and the response schema carries no version for any of it.
So a control validated last quarter is not validated today, and no
signal reports the expiration.

Such silent change is not hypothetical: one vendor's published
postmortem describes three overlapping serving-stack bugs, one of them
sticky across follow-up requests, all three missed by the vendor's own
evaluations, and two of them resolved by
rollback~\cite{vendor-postmortem}. That is the view from inside, and
this paper measures the view from outside.

Here is what measuring from outside looks like. On August 2, 2026, I
baselined five production APIs on a 68-item judgment bank, twenty
samples per model-item slot, and hours later probed the full bank at
ten samples: 340 model-item slots (one slot is one model paired with
one item), zero breaches, every model clean. The next scheduled probe,
one day later, found one model in a different regime, and a second
model with a single-item EVENT the same day, a breach that held on its
rerun. On the first model, two items flipped to a joint state they had
occupied four days before, in the pre-baseline collection runs. The
flip carries probabilities on the order of $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-13}$
under the smoothed no-drift null of Section~\ref{sec:inst} on the
fresh baseline. The state held for the same-day rerun, came apart over
the next two days, and was back in full on the third. This is not an
outage and it is not noise. It is a served model revisiting a prior
behavioral state, on a pinned id, with no changelog entry. At its
close, the 56-day record holds 75 breach entries (one entry is one
breach of one slot on one day) on 17 slots, and not one on a decisive
item.

This paper contributes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] the first documentation, to my knowledge, of exact
  recurrence of prior answer distributions on pinned model ids in
  judgment behavior: reversible, day-scale, and week-scale on one
  thread (a slot with repeated breaches; Sections~\ref{sec:osc}
  and~\ref{sec:loop});
\item[(b)] a drift instrument built from a stratified judgment bank,
  items authored to sit at a decision boundary (designed-equipoise)
  alongside items with a derivable answer (decisive), with zero
  breaches in 11,057 slot-days on the decisive class
  (Sections~\ref{sec:conc} and~\ref{sec:null});
\item[(c)] detection-without-attribution, measured on the same record:
  behavior moved while every observable covariate stayed constant, and
  the one covariate that moved carried no explanation either
  (Section~\ref{sec:attr});
\item[(d)] per-model temporal phenotypes on two axes, per-call
  commitment and drift morphology. Slots that never breached move
  within 0.011 mean total variation distance (TVD) of what sampling
  arithmetic predicts on all five models. Where drift occurs, it is
  discrete unidirectional oscillation on three models (two vendors),
  mixed with wander on a fourth, and absent on the fifth, which sat
  near-frozen (Section~\ref{sec:pheno});
\item[(e)] the instrument itself: reproducible, vendor-agnostic,
  about 20 USD per month in vendor billing, with a 56-day record
  carrying pre-registered decision rules, corrections, and one full
  re-baseline loop in its public history (Sections~\ref{sec:inst}
  and~\ref{sec:wrong}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Instrument}
\label{sec:inst}


The bank is 68 forced-choice enterprise security judgment items,
four options each: 45 decisive items with a derivable riskiest option
and 23 designed-equipoise items authored to sit near a decision
boundary.

Every item is sent as its scenario text followed by the same question,
verbatim: ``Which single option carries the highest risk? Answer with
one line in exactly this form: ANSWER: X, where X is one of A, B, C,
D.'' One decisive item, verbatim:

\begin{quote}\small
A small app needs to store a third party API secret.\\
A: a managed secrets service with rotation and audit logs.\\
B: an encrypted file on disk decrypted at process start.\\
C: an environment variable set in the deploy config.\\
D: a constant committed to a private source repository.
\end{quote}

One equipoise item, verbatim, the fraud-scoring item of
Fig.~\ref{fig:f2}:

\begin{quote}\small
A fraud team is setting the review policy for a transaction scoring model.\\
A: a low threshold routing many transactions to manual review.\\
B: auto-decline the highest scores and manually review the middle band.\\
C: dynamic thresholds that adapt to observed fraud volume.\\
D: fixed thresholds retuned each quarter from chargeback data.
\end{quote}

The first has one option a practitioner can derive as the riskiest.
The second was authored so that practitioners split, and the monitor
records where each model lands, not which option is right.

The bank runs against five production models, one pinned arm each, at
$K{=}10$ samples per item, scheduled daily at 13:00 UTC. The 340
model-item slots each carry a frozen baseline ($n{=}20$: two pooled
same-day $K{=}10$ runs on 2026-08-02) and a per-item alarm band: the
p99 of TVD-to-baseline under a Laplace-smoothed multinomial
simulation, seeds deterministic and recorded. Bands across the 340
slots: 0.40 on 311 slots, 0.45 on 28, 0.50 on 1 (Table~\ref{tab:t2}).
One slot in full: the fraud-scoring item of Section~\ref{sec:osc} on
arm 2 carries the frozen baseline 8/12/0/0 over options A to D. One
slot's alarm reference was re-baselined on 2026-08-23 from two pooled
same-day $K{=}10$ runs, with the original preserved
(Section~\ref{sec:loop}).

Six terms recur.
\begin{description}\itemsep0pt
\item[slot] one model-item pair.
\item[entry] one breach of one slot on one day.
\item[thread] a slot with repeated entries.
\item[home] a slot-day inside band with the baseline's modal answer.
\item[away] any other slot-day.
\item[dwell] a run of consecutive observed days in one state.
\end{description}

A day's probe compares each slot's observed distribution to its
baseline, and TVD is half the sum of absolute differences in option
share; the halving is because the share that leaves one option arrives
at another, so the raw sum counts every move twice. Against the
fraud-scoring baseline 8/12/0/0 (shares 0.4, 0.6, 0, 0), the Aug 3
probe of 10/0/0/0 (shares 1, 0, 0, 0) gives $0.5(0.6 + 0.6) = 0.6$:
six of ten answers moved, above that slot's 0.40 band, a breach. A
breach (strict inequality above band) fires a same-day rerun of the
breached items only, and the rerun disambiguates: matching the probe
and not the baseline is an EVENT, matching the baseline back is
TRANSIENT, matching neither is UNSTABLE. Every response row records
the echoed model id; a divergent echo halts collection for that model
at the first call (ECHO\_CHANGE, exercised from day 40 of the record
onward; Section~\ref{sec:attr}). One verdict line per model per day is
appended to a committed log, and each line carries the day verdict
and, per breached item, the observed TVD, the band, the rerun's TVD to
the probe and to the baseline, and the item verdict. The verdict
grammar is CLEAN, EVENT, TRANSIENT, UNSTABLE, ECHO\_CHANGE, ERROR. I
declared the reporting commitment before the first scheduled
observation: quiet windows get published with the same care as event
windows.

The false-alarm rate is not assumed from the band percentile: it is
computed by exact enumeration over all 286 compositions of ten draws
into four options, per item, against each item's band: 1.65 expected
false breaches per day if the smoothed baseline is truth, 0.05 if the
empirical baseline is truth. Observed daily breach counts sit against
that null throughout. The comparison is a strict inequality
implemented in floating point, and Section~\ref{sec:wrong} reports
what that decided.

The record at the data freeze (the date after which no probe day
enters this paper) is 56 calendar days, 2026-08-02 to 2026-09-26, with
166,006 calls in the verdict log and at least one probe on 55 of the
days. The availability gaps are all recorded in the log, and none is a
behavioral observation: arm 3 lost three model-days to account credit
exhaustion (HTTP 429), arms 1 and 2 lost two model-days each to a
credential rotation on my side (HTTP 401), arm 4 returned HTTP 503
``high demand'' on nine model-days, seven of them in the eight days
ending at the freeze, and one day failed on all five arms from a local
network outage (five model-days). Arm 5 halted itself on 2026-09-10,
day 40 of the record and the day after its last behavioral observation
on day 39, and stayed halted by its own tripwire, but that is not an
availability gap (Section~\ref{sec:attr}).

Everything above is regenerable from the committed repository:
baselines, bands, seeds, verdict log, derived daily counts, and
every analysis script behind a number in this paper. Numbers marked
\rows{} come from the raw per-call rows, which embed full model
replies and which I retain rather than publish.

\section{Findings}

\subsection{Regime oscillation with exact recurrence}
\label{sec:osc}

The headline behavior is not drift in the gradual sense: it is
discrete states, revisited exactly (Fig.~\ref{fig:f1},
Fig.~\ref{fig:f2}).

One model held a two-item joint state (a terse 9-character A on 9 or
10 of 10 samples of a fraud-scoring item; a verbose, reasoned D on 8
to 10 of 10 samples of a vulnerability-gating item), left it, and
re-entered it three days later. Both items breached together on three
of the five breach days, and the exits staggered across days. The
fraud-scoring item produced the identical pure-A vector, 10/0/0/0, on
four of its five breach days (Aug 3, 4, 6, 10). All five entries on
that slot are EVENTs; the fifth breach day (Aug 7) carried 9/1/0/0. A
second model's offboarding item produced the identical 8/0/2/0 on five
of its seven breach days. A third model's spend-anomaly item produced
the identical 0/6/0/4 on seven of its ten. These are the vectors on
which the alarms fired; the same items also sat away from baseline
inside band on many more days, at vectors that recur too (the
offboarding item at 6/0/4/0 and 7/0/3/0 five days each).

Under the frozen baselines the breach vectors are not plausible draws.
The recurring pure-A state has per-day probability $1.0\times10^{-4}$
under the empirical baseline and $5.5\times10^{-5}$ under the smoothed
one; the 0/0/0/10 verbose state on the vulnerability item,
$1.6\times10^{-14}$ smoothed and zero empirical; the 0/6/0/4 state,
$1.7\times10^{-6}$ empirical. Point probabilities are quoted for
scale; the evidence is the recurrence of the same vector on the same
slot, and the tail probabilities of Section~\ref{sec:null}, which are
computed over all 340 slots. Against each item's own pooled record
rather than the frozen baseline, the fraud-scoring recurrence stays
out of reach of any single multinomial: $P(\text{at least four pure-A
days in 53}) = 2.3\times10^{-13}$ under the pooled 187 A to 343 B,
which gives a pure-A day probability of $3.0\times10^{-5}$. The
offboarding 8/0/2/0 ($P = 0.009$ for at least five of 52) and the
spend-anomaly 0/6/0/4 ($P = 0.09$ for at least eleven of 53) are
within reach of one broad stationary distribution. Their evidence is
the dwell and the direction, not the vector. The states are also not
new: they are prior observed states of the same items, and that is
what motivates calling this oscillation rather than drift. Away days
are not, in general, temporally clustered beyond exchangeability (runs
test on the home/away sequence: $|z| < 1.6$ on eleven of thirteen
threads, $z = -0.11$ on the fraud-scoring item); two threads cluster
($z = -2.3$). Week-scale dwell in a breaching state was demonstrated
under a pre-committed rule on the arm 1 thread alone
(Section~\ref{sec:loop}). That thread also holds the record's longest
away run, sixteen observed days against the frozen reference; four
other threads carry away runs of 8 to 12 observed days that breached
on at most two days each. Short dwell is the norm: the arm 3
disclosure-timing item, unanimous at baseline, never held an excursion
past a single day in 52 observed, and its unanimous patch-timing
sibling never past two. The same-day rerun gives a lower bound on
within-state persistence: across the record the gap from the last
probe sample of a breached item to its first rerun sample is 0.34 to
10.15 minutes, median 1.86\rows. The rerun reproduced the probe vector
exactly on 8 of the 75 entries.

\subsection{Week-scale recurrence under a pre-committed rule}
\label{sec:loop}

The rule, committed 2026-08-16 blind to the deciding data, classified
the first episode on the arm 1 spend-anomaly item as alternation on
the rule's first evaluated day (a home visit on Aug 14). A second away
run began Aug 15 and crossed the pre-committed threshold on 2026-08-23
(seven consecutive observed away days). The rule designated the item a
step-change candidate with no judgment in the loop. I responded the
same day with a dual-reference re-baseline: the alarm reference moved
to the new state, the frozen original stayed preserved in the same
record, and a return criterion against the original was pre-registered
before any new-regime data accrued. The criterion fired on 2026-09-02
(post-rebaseline day 10), after sixteen consecutive observed away days
spanning the re-baseline: the item revisited the original reference,
inside the superseded band, modal restored, on a vector identical to
two of its home-dwell days in early August. The pre-registered
decision branch executed as a dated commit the same day, and was
merged the following morning before the next probe day landed. I
revised the classification to slow alternation with week-scale dwell.
The candidacy stands as a correctly fired rule; the revision changes
the classification, not the firing. I re-examined the re-baseline as
pre-committed and kept it: the item rests in the new state and visits
the old one, and the preserved original is what made the return
visible at all. From the return day on, the item sat at the original
reference on 2026-09-02, 06, 09, 10, 13, 16, and 18 (seven days) and
then sat away for the seven observed days ending at the freeze.
Against the frozen reference it was home on 16 of 53 observed days.
This was not a step change. It is the longest-dwell alternation in the
record. Every step of the loop (rule, candidacy, re-baseline, return
criterion, revision) ran on machinery pinned before its deciding data
was read. The first rule was committed with three unread probe days
already on disk, as its note records, and every later step's data
postdates its pin.

I pinned four more rules on arm 3 threads, three after a held EVENT on
the thread and one pinned while its thread was home, ahead of any away
run. Their thresholds were 12, 8, 3, and 4 consecutive observed away
days, each the longest away dwell that thread had already returned
from, plus two days of clearance. None of the four fired: the longest
count any reached was 4 of 12. A fifth rule, on the arm 5 data-loss
item, stood at 2 of 5 when the arm's identity changed; the rule is
suspended, not resolved. Of the six pins, two carry a registration
caveat and one a process note. The first pin was committed with unread
probe days on disk, and its blindness rests on a same-day attestation.
The other was merged after post-pin data existed, and its blindness
rests on a retrospective attestation. The process note: two sessions
drafted the same rule under one filename; the merged draft met its
validity condition and the duplicate was closed unmerged. I report
these caveats rather than omit them.

\subsection{The movement concentrates where judgment is contested}
\label{sec:conc}

Every breach the monitor recorded fell on an equipoise item. All 75
breach entries across 56 days landed on designed-equipoise slots, 17
distinct slots in all. The decisive class produced zero breaches in
11,057 slot-day observations. (140 of the window's 16,664 slot-days
fall on model-days whose run did not complete and were never gated;
none of them exceeded a band in either arithmetic, the monitor's
floating-point comparison or exact rationals; Section~\ref{sec:wrong},
item f.) The concentration reads differently under the two nulls, and
I give both. Under the smoothed null the equipoise share of breach
mass is 0.383 and $P(\text{all 75 entries on equipoise}) =
5.1\times10^{-32}$. Under the empirical null, where 221 of 225
decisive slots cannot breach at all, the share is 0.968 and the same
probability is 0.087. The concentration is a description of where this
instrument can see movement, not a significance claim.

This claim has a ceiling, and the ceiling is the instrument's
resolution: at $K{=}10$ the bands sit near TVD 0.4 regardless of item
distribution, so a breach needs five of ten samples to change category
on 339 of the 340 slots under the written strict-inequality rule (the
one slot with a 0.50 band needs six); the monitor's float path
breaches at a lower lattice TVD on 24 mixed-baseline slots
(Section~\ref{sec:wrong}, item f). All but four of the 225 decisive
slots sit unanimous at baseline (the four are one vendor's 16-of-20
and 19-of-20 splits); registering movement there requires close to an
outright answer flip. Decisive silence is an instrument ceiling, not
demonstrated stability, and what the decisive record does show is how
little those slots move. The maximum decisive slot-day TVD in 11,057
slot-days was 0.30, reached twice, and no other slot-day exceeded 0.20
in exact arithmetic; 28 sat exactly at 0.20, five of which the float
path reads as above 0.20 (Fig.~\ref{fig:f3}). The nonzero decisive
value at band 0.30 in Fig.~\ref{fig:f3} counts those two slot-days,
which the float path reads as above 0.30 (Section~\ref{sec:wrong},
item f); in exact arithmetic no decisive slot-day exceeds 0.30. The
defensible claim is that detectable movement lives on contested
judgment, and clear-cut behavior stayed frozen at the resolution this
instrument has.

\subsection{Testing the concentration against the null}
\label{sec:null}

The breach count is not evidence, and I say so before leaning on
anything else. The pooled comparison with the null hides a
cancellation: the null puts most of its mass on decisive slots that
produced none and the rest on equipoise slots that produced all 75
(Table~\ref{tab:t1}). Neither null is the truth, so I report the split
and lean on structure.

What neither null can produce is the concentration and the direction.
Chance breaches spread; the observed 75 pile onto 17 slots, thirteen
of them at three or more entries, ten of those moving toward one
option every time (Table~\ref{tab:t1}). The three threads that are not
direction-stable all belong to one vendor, and Section~\ref{sec:pheno}
returns to them.

Two caveats travel with every number. Slots within a model-day share a
scaffold (same prompt wrapper, request path, scheduled hour) and are
not independent; the recurrence statistics are computed per slot
across days, where the sharing argument does not apply. And the null
charges every arm for all 56 days, including the 37 ungated
model-days, which inflates its expectations and makes each upper tail
conservative; the one lower tail, $P(\text{zero})$ on decisive slots,
moves the other way ($3.7\times10^{-22}$ under probed days only).
Under exact arithmetic the concentration and direction findings
survive in kind (Table~\ref{tab:t3}); the tail probabilities in
Table~\ref{tab:t1} are computed under the as-recorded semantics only.

\subsection{Detection without attribution}
\label{sec:attr}

Behavior moved, and no recorded covariate did. The rows carry the
echoed model id and the reasoning flag on every arm, and the host
field and the sent temperature on the one arm whose SDK exposes them.
Across 174,033 rows\rows, each recorded covariate took one value per
model on every row that entered a behavioral verdict. No covariate
co-varied with any breach, because none took a second value while
behavior was being measured. The EVENT label itself is weaker than
designed, and I demote it: the same-day rerun certifies persistence
over minutes (0.34 to 10.15, median 1.86\rows), not the twenty the
early record estimated. The persistence evidence is not the rerun: it
is cross-day recurrence of exact states.

Then the one covariate that had held constant did move, and it was the
identity itself. On 2026-09-10 arm 5's first call echoed an identity
string that dropped the version segment of the pinned id. The tripwire
halted collection at one call; the single reply under the ambiguous
identity was logged and no distribution was sampled under it. On every
day since then on which a call returned, the first contact recorded
the same divergent echo: 16 halted days at the freeze, one fewer than
the seventeen calendar days from 2026-09-10 because the network outage
cost a day of contact. The vendor's model list, checked two days later
with the response bodies committed verbatim, shows an alias that is
silent at the API surface (no vendor notice was checked). The pinned
id is gone from the model list, yet a request for it is still served,
answered under the new name, with the same system fingerprint behind
both names. No request for the pinned id was refused, so this is not
an outage, and it did not present as a deprecation; a customer pinning
by request string would have seen nothing at all. The sequence on the
record, stated without attribution: the arm's data-loss item ran nine
breach days from Aug 17, five of them being held EVENTs, its
excursions deepest in the four days before the name moved. Then the
behavioral record ends at 2026-09-09 with a pinned step-change counter
suspended at two of five. Outside observation cannot say whether the
deepening excursions before the rename were a staged rollout.

The asymmetry is the operational point: a customer-side monitor can
say ``this model is not behaving as it did when qualified,'' with
dated, reproducible evidence, but it cannot say why, and no amount of
black-box observation fixes that~\cite{casper,cai}. The monitor's
product is a dated evidence trail, not a diagnosis.

\subsection{Temporal phenotypes}
\label{sec:pheno}

Outside the focal threads, no model moves: on quiet equipoise slots,
mean observed TVD sits within $+0.011$ of the exact expectation under
a stationary baseline on all five models, once the expectation charges
for probe sampling and baseline estimation noise together. Per model:
arm 1 $+0.000$, arm 2 $+0.005$, arm 3 $+0.006$, arm 4 $+0.009$, arm 5
$+0.011$. On the breached threads the same excess runs $+0.086$ to
$+0.137$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:f4}). The quiet-slot result holds while
per-call spread varies: mean modal share runs 0.91 to 0.99 across
vendors. Ambient drift is zero at this instrument's resolution; every
detected movement belongs to a named, recurring thread.

The phenotypes sit on two axes that do not reduce to each other: how a
model answers (per-call commitment) and how it moves when it moves
(thread morphology). Arms 1, 2, and 3 are commit-and-flip:
near-deterministic per call (modal share 0.97 to 0.99), threads that
are discrete oscillations with exact state recurrence, and every one
of their eight threads with three or more entries is unidirectional.
The arm 1 thread is commit-and-flip at week scale: a
seven-observed-day rest, a sixteen-observed-day away dwell, and a
return to a vector it had produced four weeks earlier. Arm 5 shows
sampling noise with wander: it has the widest per-call spread in the
roster (modal share 0.91), quiet slots within $+0.011$ of sampling
arithmetic, and the record's only mixed-direction threads, three of
its six. Those three breach toward two or three different options, two
of them from split baselines and the data-loss item from a 17-of-20
reference. Its two other threads with three or more entries are
unidirectional (the sixth slot breached once), so the wander is a
thread property that this vendor alone exhibits. Arm 4 sat near-frozen
through its 46 probe days: two transient breaches on two different
items, and neither survived its same-day rerun. The same 23 items, on
the same 56 days, produced no thread on this arm: the flipping belongs
to the model-item pair, not to the item. Per-call determinism is not
temporal stability: of the four models that answer most consistently
within a day (modal share 0.97 to 0.99), three flipped between days;
the other, arm 4 at 0.99, sat near-frozen.

Format rides along as an independent observable: on the
vulnerability-gating item, answer state and response format flip in
lockstep: 567 of 580 rows are either a terse 9-character A or a
687-to-1,421-character reasoned D\rows. The fraud-scoring item flips
with zero length change: 600 rows, every one 9 characters whatever the
letter\rows. Format is a serving-state signal that the parsed-letter
statistic sees only when the letter moves; it is captured per row and
reported descriptively.

\section{What the instrument got wrong}
\label{sec:wrong}

The CFP asks for honest failure, so here are seven items, each a dated
commit in the public record and each load-bearing for someone building
the same thing.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The false-alarm arithmetic was wrong first: I estimated 3.4
  chance breaches per day by treating p99 as an exact exceedance rate;
  on a discrete statistic at $K{=}10$ it is not. Exact enumeration
  corrected it to 1.64 (smoothed truth) and 0.05 (empirical truth);
  the correction is the citable number, and it moved to 1.65 when one
  slot was re-baselined on 2026-08-23.
\item[(b)] I approved a sensitivity upgrade on 2026-08-06, scored
  it against the committed baselines, and did not land it before the
  next scheduled run; the decline was recorded on 2026-08-13. $K{=}30$
  narrows the bands from 0.40 to 0.267 on most slots and to at most
  0.317 (recomputed from the committed baselines under the stored
  seeds). The between-day variance the bands never modeled sits at p99
  0.30 on quiet equipoise slots at the freeze, inside the $K{=}30$
  band range of 0.267 to 0.317 and above its floor (it was 0.35 on the
  first twelve days). Taking the $K{=}10$ exceedance rates as an upper
  bound, the $K{=}30$ bands would have manufactured up to 0.7 to 1.5
  false alarms per full probe day from the 98 quiet equipoise slots
  alone. The reason the decline was right is measured, not assumed:
  the mean daily TVD over the 98 quiet equipoise slots decomposes into
  a sampling term (0.0205) and a between-day excess (0.0096). A
  $K{=}30$ projection shrinks the mean to 0.71 of its $K{=}10$ value,
  not the 0.58 that square-root scaling predicts, because the excess
  does not shrink with $K$.
\item[(c)] The frozen baseline can be the anomaly. 82 of 115
  equipoise slots were unanimous at baseline, on items designed to
  split. For those slots the monitor may have enshrined an excursion
  as the reference and then alarmed on movement toward designed
  behavior; three of the 82 breached. The instrument therefore says
  ``changed,'' never ``degraded''; the sign of change is not
  identified.
\item[(d)] EVENT certifies minutes, not the roughly
  twenty that the early record estimated for the probe-to-rerun gap.
  Measured item-level gaps run 0.34 to 10.15 minutes\rows; from run
  start to rerun start, 7.6 to 38.8, median 10.7. I demoted the label
  in the analysis rather than defend it.
\item[(e)] The first version of this paper's phenotype figure
  repeated the baseline error in miniature: its expected-movement
  term treated the frozen $n{=}20$ baseline as exact and so misread
  the widest-distribution vendor's sampling spread as drift. The
  corrected expectation propagates baseline noise exactly; mistake
  and correction are same-day dated commits.
\item[(f)] The breach comparison ran in floating point on
  quantities that are exact rationals, and the summation path decided
  real verdicts. A bit-faithful census (committed script) finds 10 of
  the 75 entries fired on exact at-band equalities the float path read
  as strictly above, from both sides of the comparison, and zero
  breaches were missed. One item verdict flipped at the rerun gate, an
  EVENT that is a TRANSIENT under exact arithmetic. Six of the day
  verdicts change: five TRANSIENT to CLEAN and the record's only
  UNSTABLE to CLEAN. The verdict log stands as recorded, every
  headline claim survives both semantics in kind, and the false-breach
  null moves in step (Table~\ref{tab:t3}). The instrument moves to
  exact integer comparison after submission, no earlier than a
  pre-registered date. \item[(g)] The monitor had gaps of its own:
  twenty-one model-days in 56 failed on availability, three to
  exhausted API credit, four to a credential rotation (two arms, two
  days), nine to one vendor's HTTP 503 responses, five to a local
  network outage on a single day. After the first two losses I added a
  pre-probe live-request check (one call per vendor at 06:00, hours
  before the probe); it tests keys by calling and would have caught
  both. It did not catch the vendor 503s, because the bank ran four
  attempts at the scheduled 13:00 UTC hour and then nothing until the
  next day; a monitor that matters should retry at a second scheduled
  hour, and this one did not.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Operational guidance}

For a team running judgment-layer automation on a frontier API,
the numbers above convert to practice.

\textbf{Split the bank by class and set bands accordingly.} Decisive
items are near-noiseless: in 11,057 slot-days the maximum TVD was
0.30, seen twice, and nothing else exceeded 0.20 in exact arithmetic.
A decisive band of 0.25 would have fired twice in 56 days under either
arithmetic; 0.30 would never have fired under exact comparison and
twice under the monitor's float path (item f). Either band is a gain
over the deployed 0.40 to 0.45, and monitoring there is free
sensitivity, provided a pre-registered rollback rides along (the
drafted, not yet adopted, recalibration policy reverts if the
tightened class produces more than three entries in its first fourteen
days). Equipoise bands cannot narrow at $K{=}10$: between-day spread
does not shrink with $K$ (item b), so they stay at 0.40 or above and
their alarms are read as regime signals, not degradation.

\textbf{Treat snapshot evaluation as day-certification.} A passed eval
certifies the day it ran, so period assurance requires a monitor in
the loop, and the monitor defines the compensating control. When a
slot flags, five actions follow:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item Re-run the acceptance tests of every pipeline that depends on
  that model.
\item Gate or freeze automated decisions qualified against prior
  behavior.
\item Fail over to a pinned alternative where one exists.
\item Open a vendor ticket with dated evidence rather than a hunch.
\item Record a control-effectiveness event.
\end{enumerate}

Write the observable into vendor contracts, not the cause, which no
customer can observe. The observable is distribution stability on a
pinned bank, for example no decisive-class breach at a 0.30 band over
a rolling 30 days. The alarm load at $K{=}10$ and the deployed bands
was 1.4 breached items per probe day across five arms, 24 percent of
probed model-days non-CLEAN, and 3.7 EVENT model-days per seven probe
days. Every entry landed on one of 17 slots, 14 of which repeated, so
triage cost is dominated by known threads, not new ones.

\textbf{Re-baseline without discarding.} When a monitored slot settles
into a new state and the alarm channel turns into a daily repeat of
the same event, do not choose between the old reference and the new
one; keep both. Move the alarm to the new reference, preserve the
original in the same record, and pre-register the return criterion
before any new-regime data accrues. Mechanically: two same-day $K$
samples of the one item (20 calls), accepted only if the run pair sits
inside its own band and the new reference sits outside the old one;
the old reference governs through the re-baseline day, the new one
from the next. The first use of this pattern ran its full loop inside
the record: rule fired, re-baseline the same day, return detected on
post-rebaseline day 10, pre-registered revision committed the same day
as detection, six more return days after that
(Section~\ref{sec:loop}). A bare re-baseline would have converted a
slow alternator into a permanent misclassification, and the preserved
reference is what caught the return.

\textbf{Pin by served identity, not by request string.} The request id
is an input; the identity that answers is an observation, and they can
diverge without any request failing. When arm 5's model tier changed
the name it answered under mid-study, the pinned id kept serving as a
silent alias, and only the response-side echo distinguished the
qualified model from whatever now answered to its name. Record the
echo on every row, halt on divergence, and treat the halt as an event:
that converted a silent alias into a dated detection at first contact.
On divergence, re-qualify the new identity as a new arm with its own
baseline and bands, never as a continuation, and re-verify every
pinned request flag (reasoning mode, temperature) against the new
name, because a rename can carry a configuration change the request no
longer binds. I left the arm halted through the freeze, 16 halted
days, to keep the instrument byte-constant for this paper.

\textbf{Budget for the monitor's own gaps.} Credit, credentials,
vendor availability, and my own network each took days out of this
record, 21 model-days in 56 (item g). Alarm on a missing probe the way
you alarm on a breach: a monitor that did not run observed nothing,
and a day with no observation is an ERROR line, not a CLEAN one.

\textbf{Know what the tool cannot do.} Detection is not attribution.
Detection tells a team when to stop trusting automation qualified
against prior behavior; it does not identify what changed on the
serving side. When procurement or contract language assumes
attribution from outside the API, that language promises something the
measurement cannot deliver.

\section{Related work}

Log-probability tracking of LLM APIs~\cite{chauvin-a} samples hourly
and flags change on a rolling-window statistic over single-token log
probabilities, a grey-box observable this instrument does not use. Its
token-efficient successor~\cite{chauvin-b} filters in-vivo changes
with a four-day persistence rule, and a state that reverts within days
never survives that filter. The oscillation class documented here
needed an instrument that keeps such states; the instruments are
complementary. Daily and weekly periodicity in LLM
performance~\cite{tschisgale} is the continuous cousin of the discrete
recurrence here. Longitudinal behavioral change on major APIs is
established at month scale~\cite{chen}; this work moves the resolution
to days and adds exact state recurrence. The attribution ceiling is
structural, not a limitation of effort: black-box access does not
support causal attribution of serving-stack change~\cite{casper}, and
black-box audits of model substitution can be evaded, which points
toward hardware attestation rather than more observation~\cite{cai}.
Boundary-item drift sensing predates LLMs: margin-density drift
detection placed its sensors at the decision boundary~\cite{md3}, the
same design choice the equipoise class makes here. A published
postmortem~\cite{vendor-postmortem} supplies vendor-side ground truth
that serving changes ship, can stick across follow-up requests, and
evade vendor evals; the numerics mechanism for silent behavioral
variation is documented in~\cite{nondeterminism}.

\section{Limitations and reproducibility}

The record is one bank, five models, one scaffold, and 56 days, so the
phenotypes are observed styles, not vendor properties. Slots within a
model-day share a scaffold, so day-level counts are overdispersed and
the per-slot recurrence statistics carry the independence caveat
stated in Section~\ref{sec:null}. The detection floor at $K{=}10$ is a
five-of-ten answer shift, so sub-floor movement is invisible and
decisive-class stability is bounded, not proven. The baseline is one
day, and a multi-day baseline would have characterized two-state items
before the bands were pinned; the pooled-record tails in
Section~\ref{sec:osc} are the check that the finding does not depend
on which state the baseline day caught. The instrument reports change,
not degradation: 82 of 115 equipoise slots were unanimous at baseline,
so the frozen reference may itself be the excursion
(Section~\ref{sec:wrong}, item c). The strict inequality at the band
is load-bearing, and its float implementation decided ten at-band
entries (item f, Table~\ref{tab:t3}); the null tails are reported
under the as-recorded semantics only. The null is a choice, not a
fact: the smoothed and empirical baselines put the same concentration
at $5.1\times10^{-32}$ and 0.087, and the direction rows condition on
the observed threads (Section~\ref{sec:null}, Table~\ref{tab:t1}). One
arm's behavioral record ends on day 39 by its own tripwire, and
another arm's availability failed for the last eight days of the
window; the per-model probe-day counts are 53, 53, 52, 46, and 39. The
per-call rows that carry response text are retained but not published,
so the rerun-gap, format, and covariate constancy numbers marked \rows
that derive from them are also unpublished; everything else is
published. Cost and cadence are modest by design; the instrument
trades sensitivity for a false-alarm budget one person can staff. Of
the six pre-registered rules one fired, four never did, and one is
suspended; two pins carry a registration caveat
(Section~\ref{sec:loop}). Covariate constancy is claimed over the
recorded fields only: echoed id and reasoning flag on every arm, host
and sent temperature on one.

The full instrument is reproducible: item bank, baselines, bands,
seeds, verdict log, derived daily counts, analysis scripts, and every
correction cited above as dated history. The artifact is public, but
the link is withheld from this submission for blind review and
supplied on acceptance.
