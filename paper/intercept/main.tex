\title{Judgment-Layer Drift, No Changelog:\\
56 Days Monitoring Five LLM APIs}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Blind submission}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Defenders track}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The behavior of a served LLM changes behind a stable model id, and the
change has a shape. I ran a calibrated behavioral monitor against five
production LLM APIs daily for 56 days. The monitor is a fixed 68-item
security judgment bank, $K{=}10$ samples per item against a frozen
baseline, simulation-calibrated alarm bands, and a same-day rerun on
every breach. Three findings. First, judgment behavior oscillates
rather than drifts: models revisit exact prior answer distributions on
contested items, one item returning to the identical unanimous vector
on four of its five breach days. Second, the movement concentrates
where judgment is contested. All 75 breach entries landed only on the
23 items authored so that practitioners split; the 45 items with a
derivable riskiest option produced no breach in 11,057
model-item-days. The breach count alone is not the evidence: the null
expects 92 breaches in 56 days and the record holds 75. The structure
is the evidence. Chance breaches would scatter across items and push
in no particular direction. Yet these pile onto thirteen items where
the null expects one, and on ten of those thirteen every breach moved
toward the same option, where the null expects 1.6. That is not noise
crossing a threshold. It is the same items switching, repeatedly,
between the same two answer states. Third, every serving covariate
visible from outside held constant while behavior moved, except the
identity string itself: one vendor aliased the pinned id mid-study
with no signal at the API surface, and only the response-side echo
caught it. Even that was a detection, not an explanation. From outside
the API, a customer can detect that a served model changed, but they
cannot say why. A snapshot evaluation certifies the day it ran, not
the period it is cited for. The monitor bills about 20 USD per month.
The instrument, the record, the analysis, and every correction are
reproducible from a public artifact, apart from the row-level figures
marked in the text.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An agent pipeline is qualified against the model serving it, and
nothing announces when that stops being true. Vendors change weights,
quantization, serving configuration, and safety layers behind stable
model ids, and the response schema carries no version for any of it.
So a control validated last quarter is not validated today, and no
signal reports the expiration.

The gap is not hypothetical: one vendor's published postmortem
describes three overlapping serving-stack bugs, one of them sticky
across follow-up requests, all three missed by the vendor's own
evaluations, and two of them resolved by
rollback~\cite{vendor-postmortem}. That is the view from inside, and
this paper measures the view from outside.

Here is what measuring from outside looks like. On August 2, 2026, I
baselined five production APIs on a 68-item judgment bank, twenty
samples per slot, and probed the full bank at ten samples hours later:
340 model-item slots, zero breaches, every model clean. The next
scheduled probe, one day later, found one model in a different regime,
and a second model with a single-item EVENT the same day, a breach
that held on its rerun. On the first model, two items flipped to a
joint state they had occupied four days before, in the pre-baseline
collection runs. The flip carries probabilities on the order of
$10^{-5}$ to $10^{-13}$ under the smoothed null on the fresh baseline.
The state held for the same-day rerun, came apart over the next two
days, and was back in full on the third. This is not an outage and it
is not noise. It is a served model revisiting a prior behavioral
state, on a pinned id, with no changelog entry. At the close of the
56-day record it holds 75 breach entries on 17 slots, and not one on a
decisive item.

This paper contributes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] the first documentation, to my knowledge, of exact
  recurrence of prior answer distributions on pinned model ids in
  judgment behavior: reversible, day-scale, and on one thread
  week-scale;
\item[(b)] a drift instrument built from a stratified judgment
  bank, designed-equipoise items alongside decisive ones, with an
  11,057 slot-day zero-breach null on the decisive class; \item[(c)]
  detection-without-attribution measured jointly: behavior moved while
  every observable covariate held constant, and the one covariate that
  moved carried no explanation either;
\item[(d)] per-model temporal phenotypes on two axes, per-call
  commitment and drift morphology. Quiet slots move within 0.011 mean
  TVD of what sampling arithmetic predicts on all five models. Where
  drift occurs, it is discrete unidirectional oscillation on three
  models (two vendors), mixed with wander on a fourth, and absent on
  the fifth, which sat near-frozen;
\item[(e)] the instrument itself: reproducible, vendor-agnostic,
  about 20 USD per month in vendor billing, with a 56-day record
  carrying pre-registered decision rules, corrections, and one full
  re-baseline loop in its public history.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Instrument}

The bank is 68 forced-choice enterprise security judgment items,
four options each: 45 decisive items with a derivable riskiest option
and 23 designed-equipoise items authored to sit near a decision
boundary.

Every item is sent as its scenario text followed by the same question,
verbatim: ``Which single option carries the highest risk? Answer with
one line in exactly this form: ANSWER: X, where X is one of A, B, C,
D.'' One decisive item, verbatim:

\begin{quote}\small
A small app needs to store a third party API secret. A: a managed
secrets service with rotation and audit logs. B: an encrypted file on
disk decrypted at process start. C: an environment variable set in the
deploy config. D: a constant committed to a private source repository.
\end{quote}

One equipoise item, verbatim, the fraud-scoring item of
Fig.~\ref{fig:f2}:

\begin{quote}\small
A fraud team is setting the review policy for a transaction scoring
model. A: a low threshold routing many transactions to manual review.
B: auto-decline the highest scores and manually review the middle
band. C: dynamic thresholds that adapt to observed fraud volume. D:
fixed thresholds retuned each quarter from chargeback data.
\end{quote}

The first has one option a practitioner can derive as the riskiest.
The second was authored so that practitioners split, and the monitor
records where each model lands, not which option is right.

Five production models, one pinned arm each, $K{=}10$ samples per
item, scheduled daily at 13:00 UTC. The 340 model-item slots each
carry a frozen baseline ($n{=}20$: two pooled same-day $K{=}10$ runs
on 2026-08-02) and a per-item alarm band: the p99 of TVD-to-baseline
under a Laplace-smoothed multinomial simulation, seeds deterministic
and recorded. Bands across the 340 slots: 0.40 on 311 slots, 0.45 on
28, 0.50 on 1 (Table~\ref{tab:t2}). One slot in full: the
fraud-scoring item of Section~\ref{sec:osc} carries the frozen
baseline 8/12/0/0 over options A to D. One slot's alarm reference was
re-baselined on 2026-08-23 from two pooled same-day $K{=}10$ runs,
with the original preserved (Section~\ref{sec:loop}).

Vocabulary, used throughout. A slot is one model-item pair. An
entry is one breach of one slot on one day. A thread is a slot with
repeated entries. A slot-day is home when it is inside band with
the baseline's modal answer, and away otherwise. Dwell is a run of
consecutive observed days in one state.

A day's probe compares each slot's observed distribution to its
baseline. TVD is half the sum of absolute differences in option share;
the halving is because share that leaves one option arrives at
another, so the raw sum counts every move twice. Against the
fraud-scoring baseline 8/12/0/0 (shares 0.4, 0.6, 0, 0), the Aug 3
probe of 10/0/0/0 (shares 1, 0, 0, 0) gives $0.5(0.6 + 0.6) = 0.6$:
six of ten answers moved, above that slot's 0.40 band, a breach. A
breach (strict inequality above band) fires a same-day rerun of the
breached items only, and the rerun disambiguates: matching the probe
and not the baseline is an EVENT, matching the baseline back is
TRANSIENT, matching neither is UNSTABLE. Every response row records
the echoed model id; a divergent echo halts collection for that model
at the first call (ECHO\_CHANGE, exercised from day 40 of the record
onward; Section~\ref{sec:attr}). One verdict line per model per day,
appended to a committed log. Each line carries the day verdict and,
per breached item, the observed TVD, the band, the rerun's TVD to the
probe and to the baseline, and the item verdict. The verdict grammar
is CLEAN, EVENT, TRANSIENT, UNSTABLE, ECHO\_CHANGE, ERROR. I declared
the reporting commitment before the first scheduled observation: quiet
windows get published with the same care as event windows.

The false-alarm rate is not assumed from the band percentile. It is
computed by exact enumeration over all 286 compositions of ten
draws into four options, per item, against each item's band: 1.65
expected false breaches per day if the smoothed baseline is truth,
0.05 if the empirical baseline is truth. Observed daily breach
counts sit against that null throughout. The comparison is a strict
inequality implemented in floating point, and Section~\ref{sec:wrong}
reports what that decided.

The record at the freeze is 56 calendar days, 2026-08-02 to
2026-09-26, with 166,006 calls in the verdict log and at least one
probe on 55 of the days. Availability gaps, all recorded in the log
and none a behavioral observation: one vendor arm lost three days
to account credit exhaustion (HTTP 429). The two arms on a second
vendor lost two days to a credential rotation on my side (HTTP
401). A third vendor's arm returned HTTP 503 ``high demand'' on
nine days, seven of them in the eight ending at the freeze. One day
failed on all five arms from a local network outage. Separately,
and not an availability gap: a fifth arm halted itself on day 40 by
its own tripwire and stayed halted (Section~\ref{sec:attr}).
Everything above is regenerable from the committed repository:
baselines, bands, seeds, verdict log, derived daily counts, and
every analysis script behind a number in this paper. Numbers marked
\rows{} come from the raw per-call rows, which embed full model
replies and which I retain rather than publish.

\section{Findings}

\subsection{Regime oscillation with exact recurrence}
\label{sec:osc}

The headline behavior is not drift in the gradual sense. It is
discrete states, revisited exactly (Fig.~\ref{fig:f1},
Fig.~\ref{fig:f2}).

One model held a two-item joint state (terse 9 or 10 of 10 A on a
fraud-scoring item; verbose 8 to 10 of 10 D on a vulnerability-gating
item), left it, and re-entered it three days later. Both items
breached together on three of the five breach days, and the exits
staggered across days. The fraud-scoring item produced the identical
pure-A vector, 10/0/0/0, on four of its five breach days (Aug 3, 4, 6,
10). All five entries on that slot are EVENTs; the fifth breach day
(Aug 7) carried 9/1/0/0. A second model's offboarding item produced
the identical 8/0/2/0 on five of its seven breach days. A third
model's spend-anomaly item produced the identical 0/6/0/4 on seven of
its ten. These are the vectors on which the alarms fired; the same
items also sat away from baseline inside band on many more days, at
vectors that recur too (the offboarding item at 6/0/4/0 and 7/0/3/0
five days each).

Under the frozen baselines these are not plausible draws. The
recurring pure-A state has per-day probability $1.0\times10^{-4}$
under the empirical baseline and $5.5\times10^{-5}$ under the smoothed
one; the 0/0/0/10 verbose state on the vulnerability item,
$1.6\times10^{-14}$ smoothed and zero empirical; the 0/6/0/4 state,
$1.7\times10^{-6}$ empirical. Point probabilities are quoted for
scale; the evidence is the recurrence of the same vector on the same
slot, and the tail probabilities of Section~\ref{sec:null}, which are
computed over all 340 slots. Against each item's own pooled record
rather than the frozen baseline, the fraud-scoring recurrence stays
out of reach of any single multinomial: $P(\text{at least four pure-A
days in 53}) = 2.3\times10^{-13}$ under the pooled 187 A to 343 B,
which gives a pure-A day probability of $3.0\times10^{-5}$. The
offboarding 8/0/2/0 ($P = 0.009$ for at least five of 52) and the
spend-anomaly 0/6/0/4 ($P = 0.09$ for at least eleven of 53) are
within reach of one broad stationary distribution. Their evidence is
the dwell and the direction, not the vector. The states are also not
new: they are prior observed states of the same items, and that is
what motivates calling this oscillation rather than drift. Away days
are not, in general, temporally clustered beyond exchangeability (runs
test on the home/away sequence: $|z| < 1.6$ on eleven of thirteen
threads, $z = -0.11$ on the fraud-scoring item); two threads cluster
($z = -2.3$). Week-scale dwell in a breaching state was demonstrated
under a pre-committed rule on the arm 1 thread alone
(Section~\ref{sec:loop}). That thread also holds the record's longest
away run, sixteen observed days against the frozen reference; four
other threads carry away runs of 8 to 12 observed days that breached
on at most two days each. Short dwell is the norm: the arm 3
disclosure-timing item, unanimous at baseline, never held an excursion
past a single day in 52 observed, and its unanimous patch-timing
sibling never past two. The same-day rerun gives a lower bound on
within-state persistence. Across the record the gap from the last
probe sample of a breached item to its first rerun sample is 0.34 to
10.15 minutes, median 1.86\rows. The rerun reproduced the probe vector
exactly on 8 of the 75 entries.

\subsection{The pre-committed step-change rule, and one full loop}
\label{sec:loop}

The rule, committed 2026-08-16 blind to the deciding data, resolved
the first episode on the arm 1 spend-anomaly item as alternation on
its first evaluated day (a home visit on Aug 14). A second away run
began Aug 15 and crossed the pre-committed threshold on 2026-08-23:
seven consecutive observed away days. The rule designated the item a
step-change candidate with no judgment in the loop. I responded the
same day with a dual-reference re-baseline: the alarm reference moved
to the new state, the frozen original stayed preserved in the same
record, and a return criterion against the original was pre-registered
before any new-regime data accrued. The criterion fired on
post-rebaseline day 10. On 2026-09-02, after sixteen consecutive
observed away days spanning the re-baseline, the item revisited the
original reference: inside the superseded band, modal restored, on a
vector identical to two of its early home-dwell days four weeks prior.
The pre-registered branch executed as a dated commit the same day, and
was merged the following morning before the next probe day landed. I
revised the classification to slow alternation with week-scale dwell.
The candidacy stands as a correctly fired rule; the revision changes
the classification, not the firing. I re-examined the re-baseline as
pre-committed and kept it: the item rests in the new state and visits
the old one, and the preserved original is what made the return
visible at all. From the return day on, the item sat at the original
reference on seven days through Sep 18 (Sep 2, 6, 9, 10, 13, 16, 18)
and then sat away for the seven observed days ending at the freeze.
Against the frozen reference it was home on 16 of 53 observed days.
This was not a step change. It is the longest-dwell alternation in the
record. Every step of the loop (rule, candidacy, re-baseline, return
criterion, revision) ran on machinery pinned before its deciding data
was read. The first rule was committed with three unread probe days
already on disk, as its note records, and every later step's data
postdates its pin.

I pinned four more rules on arm 3 threads, three after a held EVENT on
the thread and one from home ahead of any away run. Their thresholds
were 12, 8, 3, and 4 consecutive observed away days, each the thread's
own longest returned-from dwell plus two days of clearance. None
fired: the longest count any reached was 4 of 12. A fifth rule, on the
arm 5 data-loss item, stood at 2 of 5 when the arm's identity changed;
the rule is suspended, not resolved. Of the six pins, two carry a
registration caveat and one a process note. The first pin was
committed with unread probe days on disk, and its blindness rests on a
same-day attestation. One was merged after post-pin data existed, and
its blindness rests on a retrospective attestation. The process note:
two sessions drafted the same rule under one filename; the merged
draft met its validity condition and the duplicate was closed
unmerged. I report the grades rather than hide them.

\subsection{The movement concentrates where judgment is contested}
\label{sec:conc}

Every breach the monitor recorded is an equipoise item. All 75
breach entries across 56 days landed on designed-equipoise slots,
17 distinct slots in all. The decisive class produced zero breaches
in 11,057 slot-day observations. (140 of the window's 16,664
slot-days fall on model-days whose run did not complete and were
never gated; none of them exceeded a band in either arithmetic.)
The concentration reads differently under the two nulls, and I
give both. Under the smoothed null the equipoise share of breach
mass is 0.383 and $P(\text{all 75 entries on equipoise}) =
5.1\times10^{-32}$. Under the empirical null, where 221 of 225
decisive slots cannot breach at all, the share is 0.968 and the
same probability is 0.087. The concentration is a description of
where this instrument can see movement, not a significance claim.

The claim needs its ceiling stated. At $K{=}10$ the bands sit near TVD
0.4 regardless of item distribution. A breach therefore needs five of
ten samples to change category on 339 of the 340 slots under the
written strict-inequality rule (the one slot with a 0.50 band needs
six); the float path breaches at a lower lattice TVD on 24
mixed-baseline slots (Section~\ref{sec:wrong}, item f). All but four
of the 225 decisive slots sit unanimous at baseline (the four are one
vendor's 16-of-20 and 19-of-20 splits); registering movement there
requires close to an outright answer flip. Decisive silence is an
instrument ceiling, not demonstrated stability. What the decisive
record does show is how little those slots move. The maximum decisive
slot-day TVD in 11,057 slot-days was 0.30, reached twice, and no other
slot-day exceeded 0.20 in exact arithmetic; 28 sat exactly at 0.20,
five of which the float path reads as above 0.20 (Fig.~\ref{fig:f3}).
The defensible claim is that detectable movement lives on contested
judgment, and clear-cut behavior stayed frozen at the resolution this
instrument has.

\subsection{Structure against the honest null}
\label{sec:null}

The breach count is not evidence, and I will say so before leaning
elsewhere. The smoothed null expects 92.1 entries in 56 days, and the
pooled comparison with the observed 75 ($P(\text{at least }75) =
0.97$) hides a cancellation: the null puts 56.9 of its entries on
decisive slots that produced none ($P(\text{zero}) =
1.7\times10^{-25}$) and 35.3 on equipoise slots that produced 75
($P(\text{at least }75) = 3.5\times10^{-9}$). The null overcharges
unanimous slots for a prior that their frozen distributions may not
deserve, and the empirical null, which treats an $n{=}20$ estimate as
exact, expects 3.0 entries in all. Neither is the truth, so I report
the split and lean on structure.

What neither null can produce is the structure (Table~\ref{tab:t1}).
Chance breaches spread across slots; the smoothed null expects its 92
entries on about 80 distinct slots. The observed 75 entries pile onto
17. Thirteen slots carry three or more entries each, against 1.04
expected slots at that depth ($P = 6.7\times10^{-11}$); seven carry
five or more, against 0.0063 ($P = 4.4\times10^{-20}$). Direction is
structured too. Given the thirteen threads and their entry counts, the
smoothed null expects 1.6 of them to move toward the same option at
every breach; ten do ($P = 6.8\times10^{-10}$), and five of the seven
deepest do against 0.7 expected ($P = 3.3\times10^{-7}$). The three
threads that are not direction-stable all belong to one vendor, and
Section~\ref{sec:pheno} returns to them. Two caveats travel with every
number here. Slots within a model-day share a scaffold (the same
prompt wrapper, request path, and scheduled hour) and are not
independent; the recurrence statistics are computed per-slot across
days, where the sharing argument does not apply. And the smoothed null
charges every arm for all 56 days, including the 37 model-days whose
run did not complete and were never gated, which inflates its
expectations and makes each upper tail conservative; the one lower
tail, $P(\text{zero})$ on decisive slots, moves the other way
($3.7\times10^{-22}$ under probed days only). Under exact rather than
floating-point arithmetic (Section~\ref{sec:wrong}, item f) the record
reads 65 entries on 16 slots, still all equipoise, ten threads at
three or more entries (eight unidirectional) and five at five or more
(four unidirectional). The concentration and direction findings
survive in kind; the tail probabilities above are computed under the
as-recorded semantics only.

\subsection{Detection without attribution}
\label{sec:attr}

Behavior moved. No recorded covariate did. The rows carry the echoed
model id and the reasoning flag on every arm, and the host field and
the sent temperature on the one arm whose SDK exposes them. Across
174,033 rows\rows, each recorded covariate took one value per model on
every row that entered a behavioral verdict. No covariate co-varied
with any breach, because none took a second value while behavior was
being measured. The EVENT label itself is weaker than designed, and I
demote it: the same-day rerun certifies persistence over minutes (0.34
to 10.15, median 1.86\rows), not the twenty the early record
estimated. The persistence evidence is not the rerun. It is cross-day
recurrence of exact states.

Then the one covariate that had held constant did move, and it was the
identity itself. On 2026-09-10 arm 5's first call echoed an identity
string that dropped the version segment of the pinned id. The tripwire
halted collection at one call; the single reply under the ambiguous
identity was logged and no distribution was sampled under it. On every
day since then on which a call returned, the first contact recorded
the same divergent echo: 16 halted days at the freeze. The vendor
surface, checked two days later with the response bodies committed
verbatim, shows an alias that is silent at the API surface (no vendor
notice was checked). The pinned id is gone from the model list, yet a
request for it still serves, answered under the new name, with the
same system fingerprint behind both names. No request for the pinned
id was refused. This is not an outage, and it did not present as a
deprecation; a customer pinning by request string would have seen
nothing at all. The sequence on the record, stated without
attribution: the arm's data-loss item ran nine breach days from Aug
17, five of them being held EVENTs, its excursions deepest in the four
days before the name moved. Then the behavioral record ends at
2026-09-09 with a pinned step-change counter suspended at two of five.
Outside observation cannot say whether that escalation was a staged
rollout.

The asymmetry is the operational point. A customer-side monitor can
say ``this model is not behaving as it did when qualified,'' with
dated, reproducible evidence, but it cannot say why, and no amount of
black-box observation fixes that~\cite{casper,cai}. The monitor's
product is a dated evidence trail, not a diagnosis.

\subsection{Temporal phenotypes}
\label{sec:pheno}

Outside the focal threads, no model moves. On quiet equipoise slots,
mean observed TVD sits within $+0.011$ of the exact expectation under
a stationary baseline on all five models, once the expectation charges
for probe sampling and baseline estimation noise together. Per model:
arm 1 $+0.000$, arm 2 $+0.005$, arm 3 $+0.006$, arm 4 $+0.009$, arm 5
$+0.011$. On the breached threads the same excess runs $+0.086$ to
$+0.137$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:f4}). That holds while per-call spread varies:
mean modal share runs 0.91 to 0.99 across vendors. Ambient drift is
zero at this instrument's resolution; every detected movement belongs
to a named, recurring thread.

The phenotypes are two axes that do not reduce to each other: how a
model answers (per-call commitment) and how it moves when it moves
(thread morphology). Arms 1, 2, and 3 are commit-and-flip:
near-deterministic per call (modal share 0.97 to 0.99), threads that
are discrete oscillations with exact state recurrence, and every one
of their eight threads with three or more entries is unidirectional.
The arm 1 thread is commit-and-flip at week scale: a
seven-observed-day rest, a sixteen-observed-day away dwell, and a
return to a vector it had produced four weeks earlier. Arm 5 is
sampling noise with wander: it has the widest per-call spread in the
roster (modal share 0.91), quiet slots within $+0.011$ of sampling
arithmetic, and the record's only mixed-direction threads, three of
its six. Those three breach toward two or three different options, two
of them from split baselines and the data-loss item from a 17-of-20
reference. Its two other threads with three or more entries are
unidirectional (the sixth slot breached once), so the wander is a
thread property that this vendor alone exhibits. Arm 4 sat near-frozen
through its 46 probe days: two transient breaches on two different
items and nothing held. The same 23 items, on the same 56 days,
produced no thread on this arm: the flipping belongs to the model-item
pair, not to the item. Per-call determinism is not temporal stability.
Of the four models that answer most consistently within a day (modal
share 0.97 to 0.99), three flipped between days; the other, arm 4 at
0.99, sat near-frozen.

Format rides along as an independent observable. On the
vulnerability-gating item, answer state and response format flip in
lockstep: 567 of 580 rows are either a terse 9-character A or a
687-to-1,421-character reasoned D\rows. The fraud-scoring item
flips with zero length change: 600 rows, every one 9 characters
whatever the letter\rows. Format is a serving-state signal the
parsed-letter statistic only sees when the letter moves; it is
captured per row and reported descriptively.

\section{What the instrument got wrong}
\label{sec:wrong}

The CFP asks for honest failure. Seven items, each a dated commit
in the public record, each load-bearing for someone building the
same thing.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The false-alarm arithmetic was wrong first. I estimated 3.4
  chance breaches per day by treating p99 as an exact exceedance rate;
  on a discrete statistic at $K{=}10$ it is not. Exact enumeration
  corrected it to 1.64 (smoothed truth) and 0.05 (empirical truth);
  the correction is the citable number, and it moved to 1.65 when one
  slot was re-baselined on 2026-08-23.
\item[(b)] I approved a sensitivity upgrade on 2026-08-06, scored
  it against the committed baselines, and did not land it before the
  next scheduled run; the decline was recorded on 2026-08-13. $K{=}30$
  narrows the bands from 0.40 to 0.267 on most slots and to at most
  0.317 (recomputed from the committed baselines under the stored
  seeds). The between-day variance the bands never modeled sits at p99
  0.30 on quiet equipoise slots at the freeze, inside that range and
  above its floor (it was 0.35 on the first twelve days). At $K{=}10$
  exceedance rates, an upper bound on what those bands would have
  manufactured is 0.7 to 1.5 false alarms per full probe day from the
  98 quiet equipoise slots alone. The reason is measured, not assumed:
  the mean daily TVD over the 98 quiet equipoise slots decomposes into
  a sampling term (0.0205) and a between-day excess (0.0096). A
  $K{=}30$ projection shrinks the mean to 0.71 of its $K{=}10$ value,
  not the 0.58 that square-root scaling predicts, because the excess
  does not shrink with $K$.
\item[(c)] The frozen baseline can be the anomaly. 82 of 115
  equipoise slots were unanimous at baseline, on items designed to
  split. For those slots the monitor may have enshrined an excursion
  as the reference and then alarmed on movement toward designed
  behavior; three of the 82 breached. The instrument therefore says
  ``changed,'' never ``degraded''; the sign of change is not
  identified.
\item[(d)] EVENT certifies minutes, not the roughly
  twenty that the early record estimated for the probe-to-rerun gap.
  Measured item-level gaps run 0.34 to 10.15 minutes\rows; from run
  start to rerun start, 7.6 to 38.8, median 10.7. I demoted the label
  in the analysis rather than defend it.
\item[(e)] The first version of this paper's phenotype figure
  repeated the baseline error in miniature: its expected-movement
  term treated the frozen $n{=}20$ baseline as exact and so misread
  the widest-distribution vendor's sampling spread as drift. The
  corrected expectation propagates baseline noise exactly; mistake
  and correction are same-day dated commits.
\item[(f)] The breach comparison ran in floating point on
  quantities that are exact rationals, and the summation path decided
  real verdicts. A bit-faithful census (committed script) finds 10 of
  the 75 entries fired on exact at-band equalities the float path read
  as strictly above, from both sides of the comparison. Zero breaches
  were missed. One item verdict flipped at the rerun gate, an EVENT
  that is a TRANSIENT under exact arithmetic. Six of the day verdicts
  change: five TRANSIENT to CLEAN and the record's only UNSTABLE to
  CLEAN. The verdict log stands as recorded. Every headline claim
  survives both semantics in kind (65 entries on 16 slots, all
  equipoise; ten threads at three or more entries, eight
  unidirectional), and the false-breach null moves in step (1.65 to
  1.55 expected false breaches per day, smoothed truth). The
  instrument moves to exact integer comparison after submission, no
  earlier than a pre-registered date.
\item[(g)] The monitor had gaps of its own. Twenty-one model-days
  in 56 failed on availability: three to exhausted API credit, four to
  a credential rotation (two arms, two days), nine to one vendor's
  HTTP 503 responses, five to a local network outage on a single day.
  After the first two losses I added a pre-probe live-request check
  (one call per vendor at 06:00, hours before the probe); it tests
  keys by calling and would have caught both. It did not catch the
  vendor 503s, because the bank ran four attempts at the scheduled
  13:00 UTC hour and then nothing until the next day; a monitor that
  matters should retry at a second scheduled hour, and this one did
  not.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Operational guidance}

For a team running judgment-layer automation on a frontier API,
the numbers above convert to practice.

\textbf{Split the bank by class and set bands accordingly.} Decisive
items are near-noiseless: in 11,057 slot-days the maximum TVD was
0.30, seen twice, and nothing else exceeded 0.20 in exact arithmetic.
A decisive band of 0.25 would have fired twice in 56 days under either
arithmetic; 0.30 would never have fired under exact comparison and
twice under the monitor's float path (item f). Either band is a gain
over the deployed 0.40 to 0.45, and monitoring there is free
sensitivity, provided a pre-registered rollback rides along (the
drafted, not yet adopted, recalibration policy reverts if the
tightened class produces more than three entries in its first fourteen
days). Equipoise bands cannot narrow at $K{=}10$: between-day spread
does not shrink with $K$ (item b), so they stay at 0.40 or above and
their alarms are read as regime signals, not degradation.

\textbf{Treat snapshot evaluation as day-certification.} A passed eval
certifies the day it ran. Period assurance requires a monitor in the
loop, and the monitor defines the compensating control. When a slot
flags, five actions follow. Re-run the acceptance tests of every
pipeline that depends on that model. Gate or freeze automated
decisions qualified against prior behavior. Fail over to a pinned
alternative where one exists. Open a vendor ticket with dated evidence
rather than a hunch. Record a control-effectiveness event. Write the
observable into vendor contracts, not the cause, which no customer can
observe. The observable is distribution stability on a pinned bank,
for example no decisive-class breach at a 0.30 band over a rolling 30
days. The alarm load at $K{=}10$ and the deployed bands was 1.4
breached items per probe day across five arms, 24 percent of probed
model-days non-CLEAN, and 3.7 EVENT model-days per seven probe days.
Every entry landed on one of 17 slots, 14 of which repeated, so triage
cost is dominated by known threads, not new ones.

\textbf{Re-baseline without discarding.} When a monitored slot
settles into a new state and the alarm channel turns into a daily
repeat of the same event, do not choose between the old reference
and the new one; keep both. Move the alarm to the new reference,
preserve the original in the same record, and pre-register the
return criterion before any new-regime data accrues. Mechanically:
two same-day $K$ samples of the one item (20 calls), accepted only
if the run pair sits inside its own band and the new reference sits
outside the old one; the old reference governs through the
re-baseline day, the new one from the next. The first use of this
pattern ran its full loop inside the record: rule fired, re-baseline
the same day, return detected on post-rebaseline day 10,
pre-registered revision committed the same day as detection, six
more return days after that (Section~\ref{sec:loop}). A bare
re-baseline would have converted a slow alternator into a permanent
misclassification. The preserved reference is what caught the
return.

\textbf{Pin by served identity, not by request string.} The request id
is an input; the identity that answers is an observation, and they can
diverge without any request failing. When the name that arm 5's model
tier answered under changed mid-study, the pinned id kept serving as a
silent alias, and only the response-side echo distinguished the
qualified model from whatever now answered to its name. Record the
echo on every row, halt on divergence, and treat the halt as an event:
that converted a silent alias into a dated detection at first contact.
On divergence, re-qualify the new identity as a new arm with its own
baseline and bands, never as a continuation, and re-verify every
pinned request flag (reasoning mode, temperature) against the new
name. A rename can carry a configuration change the request no longer
binds. I left the arm halted through the freeze, 16 halted days, to
keep the instrument byte-constant for this paper.

\textbf{Budget for the monitor's own gaps.} Credit, credentials,
vendor availability, and my own network each took days out of this
record, 21 model-days in 56 (item g). Alarm on a missing probe the way
you alarm on a breach. A monitor that did not run observed nothing,
and a day with no observation is an ERROR line, not a CLEAN one.

\textbf{Know what the tool cannot do.} Detection is not attribution.
It tells a team when to stop trusting automation qualified against
prior behavior; it does not identify what changed on the serving side.
When procurement or contract language assumes attribution from outside
the API, that language promises something the measurement cannot
deliver.

\section{Related work}

Log-probability tracking of LLM APIs~\cite{chauvin-a} samples hourly
and flags change on a rolling-window statistic over single-token log
probabilities, a grey-box observable this instrument does not use. Its
token-efficient successor~\cite{chauvin-b} filters in-vivo changes
with a four-day persistence rule, and a state that reverts within days
never survives that filter. The oscillation class documented here
needed an instrument that keeps such states; the instruments are
complementary. Daily and weekly periodicity in LLM
performance~\cite{tschisgale} is the continuous cousin of the discrete
recurrence here. Longitudinal behavioral change on major APIs is
established at month scale~\cite{chen}; this work moves the resolution
to days and adds exact state recurrence. The attribution ceiling is
structural, not a limitation of effort: black-box access does not
support causal attribution of serving-stack change~\cite{casper}, and
black-box audits of model substitution can be evaded, which points
toward hardware attestation rather than more observation~\cite{cai}.
Boundary-item drift sensing predates LLMs: margin-density drift
detection placed its sensors at the decision boundary~\cite{md3}, the
same design choice the equipoise class makes here. A published
postmortem~\cite{vendor-postmortem} supplies vendor-side ground truth
that serving changes ship, can stick across follow-up requests, and
evade vendor evals; the numerics mechanism for silent behavioral
variation is documented in~\cite{nondeterminism}.

\section{Limitations and reproducibility}

One bank, five models, one scaffold, 56 days. The phenotypes are
observed styles, not vendor properties. Slots within a model-day share
a scaffold, so day-level counts are overdispersed and the per-slot
recurrence statistics carry the independence caveat stated in
Section~\ref{sec:null}. The detection floor at $K{=}10$ is a
five-of-ten answer shift, so sub-floor movement is invisible and
decisive-class stability is bounded, not proven. The baseline is one
day, and a multi-day baseline would have characterized two-state items
before the bands were pinned; the pooled-record tails in
Section~\ref{sec:osc} are the check that the finding does not depend
on which state the baseline day caught. The instrument reports change,
not degradation: 82 of 115 equipoise slots were unanimous at baseline,
so the frozen reference may itself be the excursion
(Section~\ref{sec:wrong}, item c). The strict inequality at the band
is load-bearing, and its float implementation decided ten at-band
entries (item f); the entry, slot, thread, and direction counts carry
their exact-arithmetic companions from the committed census, and the
null tails are reported under the as-recorded semantics only. The null
is a choice, not a fact: the smoothed and empirical baselines put the
same concentration at $5.1\times10^{-32}$ and 0.087, and the direction
rows condition on the observed threads (Section~\ref{sec:null},
Table~\ref{tab:t1}). One arm's behavioral record ends on day 39 by its
own tripwire, and another arm's availability failed for the last eight
days of the window; the per-model probe-day counts are 53, 53, 52, 46,
and 39. The per-call rows that carry response text are retained but
not published, and therefore so are the rerun-gap, format, and
covariate constancy numbers marked \rows; everything else is
published. Cost and cadence are modest by design; the instrument
trades sensitivity for a false-alarm budget one person can staff. Of
the six pre-registered rules one fired, four never did, and one is
suspended; two pins carry a registration caveat
(Section~\ref{sec:loop}). Covariate constancy is claimed over the
recorded fields only: echoed id and reasoning flag on every arm, host
and sent temperature on one.

The full instrument is reproducible: item bank, baselines, bands,
seeds, verdict log, derived daily counts, analysis scripts, and every
correction cited above as dated history. The artifact is public, but
the link is withheld from this submission for blind review and
supplied on acceptance.
